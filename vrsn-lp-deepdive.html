Two cheap fixes touch every funnel: kill the self-contradicting shipping text on the PDPs, and stop routing advertorials into the generic Legacy PDP. Executive summary

  • Trend is up. ROAS 1.11 to 1.42 across the three windows of the period, cost per purchase down from about $97 to $71. The kills (Quiz, Listicle) were correct.
  • The biggest leak is the cart, not the click. The hook and landing step are healthy, but 64% of carts never purchase and only 8% of visitors add to cart. That is where the money is.
  • Finding A. Every PDP shows "shipping delayed 2-3 weeks" and "delivers in 5-8 days" on the same screen. A trust break at the decision point. A 10 minute fix that touches all four PDPs.
  • Finding B. All four advertorials route to the generic "VRSN - Premium Product" page and show a "$135/pair" price against a $79 entry price. Message-match breaks where the Story funnel already leaks.
  • It is page-level and ad-level, not fatigue. Ad frequency is low everywhere (1.0 to 1.3), so nothing is burnt out. Barefoot is the thinnest page and converts worst, Orthotics-Exit converts fine but its hook is weakest, and Foot Pain is being drained by a single 0.77-ROAS ad that CBO over-funded. Each fix below is tied to the number that motivates it.

2. The trend is real

Three windows, one direction. Driven by cutting the dead weight, the Nurse story maturing, and the relaunch test bedding in.

3. Where the funnel actually breaks

The hook and landing step are fine. The money is lost in two places: only 8% of visitors add to cart, then 64% of carts never buy. That second drop is the largest pooled leak on the account.
Data hygiene flag: "Checkouts initiated" fired only 77 times versus 168 purchases, which is impossible. The initiate-checkout pixel is mis-firing, so the cart to checkout to purchase split is currently blind. Cart-to-buy is used as the friction proxy throughout.

One ad is sinking the Foot Pain campaign

"Foot Pain Resignation 028" took 47% of the Foot Pain budget ($962) at 0.77 ROAS, and in the last 3 days it took 39% of spend at 0.00 ROAS (zero sales), while sibling ads 033, 026 and 007 run 1.7 ROAS or better.

Confidence: high. Cut or cap 028 and let budget flow to the profitable siblings.

6. The two systemic findings

These came from reading the live pages, not the metrics, and each is paired with the evidence behind it and an honest confidence level.
FINDING A. AFFECTS ALL 4 PDPS. 10 MINUTE FIX. HIGHEST ROI.

Every PDP contradicts itself on shipping

At the add-to-cart moment, all four PDPs show two conflicting delivery promises within a few lines of each other:

"Due to very high demand, shipping is currently delayed by 2-3 weeks"
"Ships to United States. Free shipping. Delivers in 5-8 days"

Pick one message and remove the other across every PDP.

Evidence
Read live on travel, barefoot, orthotics-exit and Legacy. Both strings sit near the buy box on all four.
Why it matters
A conflicting delivery promise at the decision point creates doubt at the exact moment of commitment. The account loses 64% of carts (470 add-to-cart, 168 purchases), the single biggest pooled leak.
Confidence: the contradiction is a verified fact. Its share of the 64% abandonment is a strong hypothesis, because checkout is not instrumented (see the broken pixel note). Validate by fixing it and watching cart-to-buy move.
FINDING B. AFFECTS ALL 4 STORIES. ATTACKS THE STORY CART LEAK.

Every advertorial hands traffic to the generic Legacy PDP

All four Story CTAs ("View full product details" and "Claim deal") link to /, the Legacy PDP, whose title is "VRSN - Premium Product" and whose hero is the generic "Kills Foot Pain."

Orthotics story ("I stopped needing orthotics") routes to the generic PDP, while a perfect-match /orthotics-exit ("Ditch the Orthotics") already exists.

Stories also show "From $135.00/pair" on the sticky bar, contradicting the $79 one-pair price.

Evidence
CTA href is "/" on all four Stories (read from the page). Story article-view to cart is 6.3% to 7.5%, below the destination PDP own cold rate of 9.2%, so readers leak between article and cart.
Why it matters
A reader primed on a specific angle lands on a generic page and a price that looks 70% higher than entry. Message-match breaks exactly where the funnel already leaks.
Confidence: the handoff and the price bug are verified facts. That a matched PDP lifts conversion is a test, not a certainty, because orthotics-exit cold cart rate (8.9%) is just under Legacy (9.2%). Run it as an A/B split.

11. Shared page-level watch-items

Lower-confidence observations that apply to every page. These are judgment calls, not proven leaks, so treat them as before/after tests rather than certain fixes.
  • Static social proof looks identical across pages. Every PDP shows the same "192 sold today" and "37 to 45 viewing" plus "sold out 7 times last year". Repeated, unchanging numbers can read as fake to returning or savvy shoppers and risk the trust they are meant to build. Consider making them dynamic or toning them down. Confidence: low.
  • Two separate countdown timers per page. A top-bar timer and a product timer show different values. Duplicated urgency can feel manufactured. Consider a single consistent timer. Confidence: low.
  • Variant defaults to Women, 1 pair, White. Good that a variant is pre-selected, but defaulting to 1 pair while the page pushes 2 pairs as "Best Value" is worth an A/B test against a 2-pair default for AOV. Confidence: medium.

12. Killed pages, confirm dead vs fixable

  • /quiz, ROAS 0.53 (off): broken at two stages (CTR 3.0% and view to cart 3.6%). The quiz adds friction without payoff. Stay off. If ever revisited, it should pre-load a recommended product into the cart at the end rather than just reveal a result.
  • /compare, ROAS 0.00, 0 purchases on $302 (off): 117 landing views, 5 carts, zero sales. Dead. Stay off.

Method and caveats

  • Metrics from the Meta daily ad-level export, 1 to 10 June 2026, aggregated so campaign equals landing page.
  • Page content read live from the rendered pages of all 10 LPs on 11 June.
  • "Page weight" (character count) is a proxy for which persuasion blocks are present. A screenshot pass would confirm visual hierarchy and above-the-fold.
  • Cart-to-buy happens in Shopify checkout, which is not visible from the LP. The shipping contradiction is the most visible suspect but a checkout walkthrough would confirm.
  • Brand fonts: body set in Figtree (brand). Headlines use Archivo as a stand-in for Silka, which is a licensed font not available here.
  • Ad frequency was checked across all campaigns (1.0 to 1.3), so overexposure fatigue is ruled out as a cause.
  • New-site TOF relaunch test excluded from all analysis per request.
