The honest caveat: why we are not killing the offer today This is not a perfectly clean A/B test. The two offers also differ in landing page, in creative format (the $89 side runs video and a "40% off" message, while the $79 side runs mostly static advertorials), and in campaign setup. And with 34 purchases on the $89 side, the gap is a strong directional signal, not yet a statistically settled one. Four measures pointing the same way earns a proper test. It does not yet earn a verdict.
The plan: how we settle this question This is what we plan to run, starting this week. A clean offer test: same landing page, same top creative (Nurse), same audience, split into two identical ad sets where the only difference is A: $79 with the comfort insole free versus B: $89 with the insole paid. We will judge it on profit per visitor, not raw conversion. Target date for the keep-or-kill decision on the free insole: June 26.

One more signal hiding in this data: the $79 funnels attract more clicks (up to 13.6% click-through on Barefoot) but close fewer of them into sales. Plenty of traffic, weak close rate. That points to a page problem, not a price problem, and it matches what the LP Deep Dive found independently: a contradictory shipping message on every product page, and story pages handing visitors to a generic page showing $135 instead of the $79 offer. Fixing those lifts every ad dollar, whatever the price ends up being.

At roughly $1.4K per day focused on three angles, that is 10 to 14 more days. Spending more per day would buy speed but muddy the read.

The plan for this week, on our side

  • Landing page fixes go live: the contradictory shipping message comes off all product pages, and story pages will send visitors to their matching product page at the $79 price instead of a generic page showing $135. This lifts every funnel at no media cost.
  • Budget moves to the winners: ads with no sales and angles below the 1.2 floor get cut, the overspending "Resignation 028" ad gets capped, and the freed budget concentrates on Nurse, Barefoot and the $89 page.
  • The clean $79 vs $89 test goes live: same page, same ads, same audience. Only the offer differs.
  • A second creative generation gets queued: the winning hooks (Nurse, Barefoot) go back into the AI engine to produce sharper variations. This tests the engine's core promise: that quality compounds round over round.
